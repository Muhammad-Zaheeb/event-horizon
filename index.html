How this works

Each pixel sends a ray of light backwards from your eye. The GPU follows its exact path around a non-spinning (Schwarzschild) black hole by stepping the orbit equation d²x/dλ² = −(3/2) h² x / r⁵ (in units where rₛ = 1), where h is the ray's angular momentum. That reproduces general relativity's light bending exactly, for every pixel, in real time.

You are a static observer hovering in place, so your view includes gravitational blueshift and the squeezing of the sky as you get close.

Where a ray crosses the disk, the gas glows as a blackbody at the Shakura–Sunyaev temperature for that radius, shifted by g = √(1 − 3M/r) / [(1 − Ωℓ)·√(1 − 2M/r_cam)]: Doppler beaming from the orbit plus gravitational redshift. Colours come from the Planck spectrum folded through the CIE colour-matching functions.

Stars and the Milky Way are lensed by the same rays. Nothing here is pre-rendered.
